About

Engineering focus

I am an Aerospace Engineering student at Texas A&M University, graduating May 2027 and continuing into an M.S. through the Fast Track BS/MS program. My work follows an aerospace vehicle across disciplines: define the load path, build the geometry, verify the stresses, close the control loop, implement the embedded software, and manage the requirements and interfaces that hold the complete system together.

Most of what I build is deliberately unglamorous, test harnesses, fault injectors, consistency checks, convergence studies, traceability matrices. That is because the interesting part of aerospace engineering is not the demo that works once. It is the evidence that it will work the next thousand times, and the honest account of the cases where it will not.

I am looking for roles in mechanical design and analysis, structural FEA, controls and estimation, flight software, embedded systems, verification, and systems engineering. The common thread is physical aerospace hardware: understanding the loads, dynamic response, software behavior, interfaces, and test results that make a design defensible.

Principles

How I work

01 / Evidence

I prefer projects with measurable evidence: tests, logs, plots, traces and reproducible procedures. Anything I claim, I can show.

02 / Interfaces

I care about the boundaries where designs break: joints, load paths, tolerances, thermal interfaces, packet formats, timing, frame conventions and fault handling.

03 / Discipline

I use projects to practise the habits expected across mechanical, flight, and systems teams: sound assumptions, verification, documentation, external review, and disciplined iteration.
