About

Engineering focus

I am an Aerospace Engineering student at Texas A&M University, graduating May 2027 and continuing into an M.S. through the Fast Track BS/MS program. My work sits at the point where aerospace theory has to become executable: estimation and control algorithms, embedded flight software, command and telemetry links, and the structural analysis that has to close before any of it is allowed to fly.

Most of what I build is deliberately unglamorous — test harnesses, fault injectors, consistency checks, convergence studies, traceability matrices. That is because the interesting part of aerospace engineering is not the demo that works once. It is the evidence that it will work the next thousand times, and the honest account of the cases where it will not.

I am looking for roles in GNC, flight software, embedded systems, simulation, verification and systems engineering — the places where software meets a physical vehicle and the consequences of being wrong are real.

Principles

How I work

01 / Evidence

I prefer projects with measurable evidence: tests, logs, plots, traces and reproducible procedures. Anything I claim, I can show.

02 / Interfaces

I care about the boundary between software and physical systems — packet formats, timing, frame conventions, fault handling and observability. That is where systems break.

03 / Discipline

I use side projects to build the habits expected in flight and autonomy work: verification, documentation, external review, and disciplined iteration.
