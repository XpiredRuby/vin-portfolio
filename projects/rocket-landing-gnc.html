Rocket Landing GNC

6-DOF DESCENT · THRUST VECTOR CONTROL · 500-RUN MONTE CARLO

A propulsive landing controller is only as good as its worst case. This project builds the 6-DOF simulation that generates the worst cases, and then answers the question a reviewer actually asks: what is the landing dispersion across the full uncertainty envelope?

01Problem

A controller that lands the booster on the nominal trajectory demonstrates almost nothing. The vehicle never flies the nominal trajectory: it separates with attitude and rate errors, it descends through a wind field nobody measured, its mass properties shift as propellant depletes, and its navigation solution has bias. The engineering question is not "does it land", it is "across the dispersion envelope, what is the landing accuracy distribution, and which specific combination of dispersions produces the tail?"

Answering that requires two things that are easy to skip: dynamics complete enough that the controller can actually fail in a physical way, and enough dispersed runs that the tail is populated rather than assumed.

Problem statement. Build a 6-DOF booster descent simulation with realistic disturbance and sensor modelling, close a thrust-vector control loop around it, and characterise landing accuracy over a statistically meaningful dispersion campaign.

03Constraints

Physical
  • Gimbal authority is finite. Deflection angle and slew rate are both limited, so the controller can and does saturate.
  • Throttle range is bounded. The engine cannot be commanded below its minimum stable thrust.
  • Propellant is finite. A controller that lands perfectly on an empty tank has not landed.
  • Aerodynamic authority disappears. As dynamic pressure decays near touchdown, the vehicle becomes almost purely thrust-controlled.
Computational
  • 500 runs must be affordable. Integrator and step size are chosen so the full campaign completes in a practical wall-clock time.
  • Runs must be reproducible. Every trial is seeded so a tail case can be re-run and inspected.
  • Pure Python + NumPy/SciPy. No compiled solver, so vectorisation carries the performance.

04System design

A conventional GNC decomposition: guidance produces a reference, control produces a command, the actuator model applies it with real limits, the plant integrates the physics, and navigation produces an estimatenot truth, which is what the controller actually sees. Wind and sensor error are injected as separate dispersion channels so their contributions can be separated in the analysis.

05Mathematical models

Rigid-body dynamics

m(t) v̇ = R{q}(T_gimbal) + F_aero + m(t) g − m(t) ω × v I(t) ω̇ = M_gimbal + M_aero − ω × (I(t) ω) q̇ = ½ q ⊗ [0, ω] ṁ = −T / (I_sp g₀) mass depletion couples straight back into m(t), I(t)

Aerodynamics

q̄ = ½ ρ(h) V² dynamic pressure, ρ from atmosphere model F_aero = q̄ S [C_D, C_Y, C_L]ᵀ coefficients functions of Mach and angle of attack M_aero = q̄ S L_ref [C_l, C_m, C_n]ᵀ

Aerothermal heating

q̇_stag ∝ ρ^0.5 V³ stagnation-point convective heating scaling Q = ∫ q̇ dt integrated heat load along the trajectory

The heating model is not used to size thermal protection here, it is used to confirm that the trajectories the guidance law selects stay inside a sensible heating envelope, rather than buying landing accuracy with an unflyable entry.

Control law

e = x_ref − x̂ error against the estimated state δ_cmd = K_p e + K_i ∫e dt + K_d ė δ = clamp(δ_cmd, ±δ_max) deflection limit δ̇ = clamp(δ̇, ±δ̇_max) slew-rate limit

Dispersion model

per trial, drawn from seeded distributions initial position, velocity, attitude, rate errors wind profile: magnitude and shear, altitude dependent sensor bias and noise on the navigation solution thrust and I_sp variation

06Algorithms used

  • Numerical integration of the coupled translational, rotational and mass states over the descent.
  • Quaternion attitude propagation with re-normalisation, avoiding the gimbal-lock singularity of an Euler-angle formulation during a high-rate descent.
  • PID with anti-windup on the thrust-vector command, necessary because the actuator saturates, and an unprotected integrator winds up during saturation and overshoots on release.
  • Standard atmosphere model for density and speed of sound versus altitude.
  • Seeded Monte Carlo sampling across the dispersion channels, with per-trial seeds retained so any outcome is reproducible.
  • Dispersion post-processing: landing scatter, CEP, and correlation of miss distance against each dispersion channel to identify which one drives the tail.

08Testing

  1. Conservation checks. With thrust, aerodynamics and dissipation disabled, energy and momentum must be conserved to integrator tolerance, this catches sign and frame errors that a plausible-looking trajectory will hide.
  2. Analytical comparison. Ballistic and constant-thrust sub-cases are compared against closed-form solutions.
  3. Step-size convergence. The integrator step is halved until the solution stops changing, which sets the step actually used.
  4. Open-loop instability check. Without control the vehicle must tumble as expected; a plant that is stable open-loop is not modelling the physics.
  5. Nominal closed-loop run. Zero dispersion, verifying the controller lands on the pad before any stochastic testing begins.
  6. Full dispersion campaign. 500 trials, with the tail cases pulled out and re-run individually.

09Validation

The Monte Carlo campaign validates the systemnot just the controller. Three things had to be true for the result to mean anything: the dispersions had to be physically justifiable rather than chosen to produce a good answer; the sample had to be large enough that the tail was populated; and the failures had to be attributable to a mechanism.

  • Convergence of the statistic. Landing-accuracy statistics were checked for stability as trial count increased, confirming 500 runs was sufficient rather than assumed sufficient.
  • Attribution. Miss distance was correlated against each dispersion channel individually, which is what separated a genuine controller weakness from an unlucky draw.
  • Saturation auditing. Every trial logged actuator saturation, so a "successful" landing that only worked because the model let the gimbal exceed its physical limit could not pass unnoticed.

  • GNC robustness validated across 500 stochastic wind runs to sub-2.0 m landing accuracy.
  • The failure tail was traced to a specific combination, strong low-altitude wind shear arriving after the controller had already committed to a correction, rather than to insufficient gain margin.
  • Actuator saturation, not control law structure, was the binding constraint in the worst cases: the controller knew what to do and could not physically do it fast enough.
  • Trajectories selected by the guidance law stayed inside a sensible aerothermal envelope, confirming accuracy was not bought with an unflyable entry profile.

12Challenges

Integrator windup during saturation

Early dispersed runs produced a distinctive failure: the vehicle would track well, saturate the gimbal during a gust, and then overshoot dramatically once the gust passed. The integral term had continued accumulating during saturation while the actuator was physically unable to respond. Anti-windup on the integrator removed the failure mode entirely, and it only appeared in the dispersed set, never in the nominal run.

Coupled mass properties

Mass, centre of mass and inertia all change as propellant depletes, which changes the moment arm of the gimballed thrust, which changes the control authority the controller assumes it has. Treating mass as constant made the controller look better than it was in exactly the final seconds that matter most.

Campaign runtime

500 6-DOF trials in interpreted Python is a real cost. Vectorising the environment and atmosphere evaluations, and choosing the integrator step from the convergence study rather than conservatively, brought the campaign into a practical runtime without changing the answer.

13Lessons learned

  • The nominal run is the least informative run. Every interesting failure in this project appeared only under dispersion.
  • Simulate what the controller can see. Feeding truth back is the single easiest way to produce an accuracy number that will not survive contact with hardware.
  • Actuator limits are part of the control design. Deflection and slew limits determined the achievable accuracy more than any gain did.
  • Attribute the tail. "500 runs, sub-2 m" is a result. "500 runs, sub-2 m, and here is the mechanism behind the worst 1%" is engineering.
  • Verify the plant before trusting the controller. Energy conservation checks caught frame errors that would have invalidated the entire campaign.

14Future work

  1. Replace the PID law with convex-optimisation-based guidance and compare dispersion directly against the current baseline.
  2. Add a proper navigation filter in place of injected estimate error, so navigation and control errors interact realistically.
  3. Model engine start transients and minimum-impulse-bit behaviour rather than treating throttle as continuous.
  4. Extend the campaign to characterise propellant margin distribution, not only landing accuracy.
  5. Port the inner loop to C++ so the campaign can grow to several thousand trials.
